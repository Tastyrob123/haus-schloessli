Non-Public Information Request Process Disclosure For privacy reasons, Squarespace Domains LLC and Squarespace Domains II LLC (collectively, “Squarespace Registrar Entities” and each, a “Squarespace Registrar Entity”) generally redact customers’ personal contact information within registration data (“non-public registration data”) associated with general top-level domains for which a Squarespace Registrar Entity serves as the registrar of record (“domain name”) when a public lookup of such registration data through Squarespace’s Registration Data Directory Services (“RDDS”) outputs are performed. However, in order to comply with ICANN’s Registration Data Policy, Squarespace Registrar Entities are obligated to allow third parties to submit a request for non-public registration data associated with a domain name and have such non-public registration data to be disclosed to them directly on the basis of demonstrable legitimate interest (each, a “request”). This page outlines (a) the format and content required for each request that must be submitted for Squarespace’s review, (b) how Squarespace will respond to your request and (c) the anticipated timeline for our response to your request. Only requests for single domains are permitted. Please note that this policy does not jeopardize the fundamental interests, rights and freedoms of a data subject, consistent with the General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR") or other similar privacy laws. It is also not a substitute for other less-intrusive mechanisms available and we expect these mechanisms to be exhausted prior to any request submitted hereunder, which request should only be submitted as an extreme, last resort measure given its potential impact to the data subject. Points to consider • If you are looking to only obtain non-public registration data in order to reach out to the contact associated with a domain name, Squarespace offers the ability to communicate through the form available here. • If you are looking to report DNS abuse for a domain name registered through Squarespace, steps on how to do so are available here. • If you believe you are potentially the victim of a crime associated to a domain name, you may file a complaint with the FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) , European Cybercrime Centre (EC3) , or if you reside outside the foregoing regions, then your region’s equivalent cybercrime reporting authority. • If you believe that a domain name registered at Squarespace violates your rights or if you believe that the domain Registrant is otherwise in breach of the representations made in Section 2 of the Uniform Domain Name Dispute Resolution Policy (UDRP), then you may file a complaint against the Registrant with an ICANN-approved dispute resolution provider (the “Resolution Provider”). You can find more information about the UDRP process, including how to file a complaint against the Registrant with an ICANN-approved dispute resolution provider, here. • If you are a law enforcement agency seeking information, please note that we are unable to provide information about Squarespace accounts, users, or sites to law enforcement without a subpoena, court order, or warrant. Once the receipt of the information and statements below have been acknowledged, Squarespace will review your submission and respond via email to the contact address provided with the request within 30 days from such acknowledgment absent exceptional circumstances. Squarespace Registrar Entity, in its sole discretion, may grant or deny requests and decisions are considered final. Repeated data access requests against our policy may result in rejection of any future requests you may submit. A failed payment is different. This is a decline issued by the shopper’s own card issuer or bank, typically due to an expired card, insufficient funds, or the issuer’s own risk checks. Squarespace cannot intervene on these declines. Please direct the shopper to contact their bank directly. Before you continue, a quick note on how this works: Our checks are designed to protect you from fraudulent transactions and the disputes that come with them. They are not perfect and sometimes a genuine customer gets caught in them by mistake, that is exactly what this form is for. If you tell us this shopper is genuine and we lift the block, please know the payment is not guaranteed to succeed. Standard dispute and chargeback rules still apply, so if the order turns out to be fraudulent after all, your store carries that risk the same as any other transaction. A few common red flags worth checking before you vouch for a shopper: Overpayment followed by a refund request, unusually large or rushed orders from a new customer, a shipping address far from the billing address, or multiple orders going to the same address under different names. Export your site content Last updated October 09, 2026 21:18 Sections Create an .xml file of your site content, then import it to WordPress. You can export certain content from your Squarespace site into an .xml file. Many features rely on our platform's JavaScript and CSS, so some content doesn't export. Note: You can't export content from one Squarespace site and import it into another. Importing content into a different Squarespace site can cause broken image links and other issues. Video: Exporting your site content This one-minute video walks through exporting your content as an .xml file and downloading it to your computer. What exports to WordPress and what doesn't Because of how WordPress is designed, you can't import every type of Squarespace content. The .xml file exports the content that imports to a WordPress site. Some content, like linked .pdf files, exports in the .xml file but can't import to a WordPress site. To learn more, visit WordPress' documentation on importing from Squarespace . Content that exports Layout pages One blog page, including all of its posts and up to 1000 comments per post Text blocks Image blocks Text from other blocks like the embed block and Instagram block will export with minimum structure Gallery pages, including York project pages ( version 7.0 only) Content that doesn't export Other page types (including album pages, cover pages, index pages, info pages, calendar pages, portfolio pages, and store pages) Content in page-specific headers, footers, and sidebars More than one blog page Dropdowns Audio blocks Product blocks Video blocks Drafts Style settings Custom CSS Export and download your content To create and download the .xml file: Ensure your site is published and your site availability is set to Public . You can't export from a trial site. Open the Import & export content panel . Click Export . Click the WordPress icon. If you’re not importing the exported content into WordPress site, click the icon anyway. The exported file can only be in a WordPress format, but you don’t have to import it into WordPress. If your site has more than one blog page, select a primary blog from the dropdown menu, then click Export . Only one blog page exports. Wait for the export to process. This can take a few minutes, depending on how much content your site has. Click Download to save the .xml file to your computer, or click Clear to erase the export. Keep your images by importing to WordPress.com If you import your content into a self-hosted WordPress site with the WordPress Importer plugin, your images might not migrate. WordPress can pull reference links for the images instead, so the image links stop working after you cancel your Squarespace site. To keep your images working, import your content into the WordPress.com version of the platform, which copies your images to WordPress' servers. After importing, confirm all your images appear in WordPress' Media Library. If images are missing, contact WordPress for more help. You can also keep using WordPress Importer and download your site's images manually, then upload them to your WordPress site. To compare both versions of WordPress, visit their documentation . Export content from an expired site If your site has expired, you might be able to recover and export your content by reactivating your subscription first. Depending on how long the site has been expired, your content may already be deleted. My Squarespace content isn’t importing into Wordpress If you're having trouble importing to WordPress, you can download or copy content like images and text directly from your Squarespace site.
